Funding

Three ways to fund a plan

Fully insured

The carrier holds the risk and sets the rate. We shop it and negotiate the renewal.

Level-funded

A fixed monthly amount covering expected claims, stop-loss and administration, with a possible surplus if claims run well.

Self-funded with stop-loss

You pay claims as they come and stop-loss reinsurance caps the exposure. We shop the contract terms, not just the premium.

Group sizes we work with

  • 1 to 50 employees — marketed through general-agency relationships that return several carriers from one submission.
  • 51 or more — submitted directly to carriers, market by market.
  • 100 or more, or any level-funded or self-funded plan — underwritten on claims experience, which we collect and send.

Group size also decides what applies: COBRA at 20 or more employees, the ACA affordability question at 50 or more.

What we do

From intake to the first invoice

  1. Take your group to market — market by market, not state by state.
  2. Put every quote on one comparison — current, renewal and each option, in the same format.
  3. Start the renewal early — marketing begins about 90 days before your effective date.
  4. Build a contribution model you can defend — employer and employee split by tier.
  5. Run open enrollment — dates, meetings, an employee benefit guide, and a Summary of Benefits and Coverage for every plan sold.
  6. Set up benefits administration — plans, rates and eligibility tested against what was sold, before anyone enrolls.
  7. Finish the carrier hand-off — sold paperwork, termination letter, deductible-credit request, enrollment census, ID cards, and the first invoice checked.
  8. Deliver a close-out packet — sold confirmation, annual disclosures, plan documents and our written compensation disclosure.
  9. Take over an existing plan mid-year — a broker-of-record change moves the account without changing your carrier, plan or rates.
Between renewals

We do not disappear after January 1

Day to day

  • Invoice and billing reconciliation against what was sold
  • Eligibility, new hires and life-event changes
  • Employee questions about their coverage
  • ID cards, and claim or billing escalations with the carrier
  • A midyear stewardship and benchmarking review

The calendar we keep for you

  • Renewal marketing kicked off about 90 days out
  • Form 5500 deadline tracking for ERISA plans at or above the participant threshold
  • PCORI (Patient-Centered Outcomes Research Institute) fee reminders
  • Medicare Part D creditable-coverage notices (October 15)
  • ERISA plan documents — wrap Summary Plan Description, Section 125 premium-only plan, Summary Annual Reports and Summaries of Material Modifications
  • COBRA administrator coordination at 20 or more employees
  • ACA affordability considerations at 50 or more

We track, prepare and remind. We are not your attorney or your accountant, and filing deadlines remain the plan sponsor's.

What we do

An incomplete submission is a slow quote, or no quote

  1. Applications complete before they go out — the standard ACORD forms each line needs, plus the carrier's own supplement where there is one.
  2. A schedule of values covering every location — limits, construction and occupancy for each building.
  3. Loss runs, currently valued — three to five years, one per policy per year.
  4. The rest of a complete file — payroll by class code, financials for management lines, your expiring applications, and a target premium.
  5. Terms reviewed against your expiring policy — deductibles, forms, sublimits and premium, before you see a proposal.
  6. Bind and issue — the bind request with every condition the carrier attaches, then the binder, certificates and evidence of insurance.
  7. Every issued policy checked — against the binder and the quote, or back to the carrier with a discrepancy list.
  8. Premium financing — shopped across finance companies when you want it.
Between renewals

The year after the policy is issued

  • Certificates of insurance and evidence of insurance on request
  • Mid-term changes and endorsements
  • Claims reporting and follow-up — we file the claim with the carrier and chase the acknowledgement, the adjuster assignment and the documents
  • Carrier inspections — most often on general liability; we stay on both stages, getting the visit scheduled and closing out the recommendations
  • Every renewal marketed against your expiring terms

Questions we get

Straight answers

Do you handle both employee benefits and commercial property & casualty?

Yes. Two practice teams, one firm, often on the same account.

What group sizes do you work with?

Groups of 1 to 50 through general-agency markets, 51 and up directly with carriers, and groups of 100 or more — or any level-funded or self-funded plan — underwritten on claims experience.

Do you work with self-funded or level-funded plans?

Yes. We compare level-funded against fully insured side by side, and for self-funded groups we shop the stop-loss contract terms and the administrator's fees, not just the premium.

Can we change brokers without changing our plan?

Yes. A broker-of-record letter moves the account to us; your carrier, plan and rates stay as they are.

What happens after open enrollment?

We stay on the account: invoices, eligibility changes, employee questions, carrier escalations, the compliance calendar, and the renewal started early.

Which industries do you serve?

Healthcare, commercial cleaning and building services, marine and yacht services, power generation, schools and nonprofits, manufacturing, and installation and specialty contractors — and others. The process is the same.
